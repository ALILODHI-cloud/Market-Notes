# JUZ27/JUZ26 (Dec 27 Sonia contract - Dec 26 Sonia contract) steepener

We are initating a steepener on the Dec 27 Dec 26 SONIA curve. The Dec 26 1-month SONIA contract currently prices 42.5bps of hikes through the end of 2026, and 46bps through the end of 2027 - giving a slope of 3.5bps. This is too flat, relative to (i) the macro backdrop and (ii) BOE communication from the April MPC. 

## (1) Macro backdrop

The prevailing macro backdrop is an unequivocally soft one. Some recent prints include:

- **Labour market overview, UK: May 2026** (ONS, 19 May): unemployment **5.0%** (Jan–Mar), up from **4.9%** (Dec–Feb); vacancies and payrolls softening. Key Policy Judgement 1 (April MPR): *"Continued weakness in demand and the labour market is likely to lessen the strength of second-round effects."*
- **Consumer price inflation, UK: April 2026** (ONS, 20 May): CPI **2.8% y/y** (from **3.3%**); CPIH services **3.4%** (from **4.3%**).
- **Citi/YouGov inflation expectations** (27 Apr): one-year-ahead **5.0%** (from **5.4%** in March).
- **S&P Global UK Services PMI** (final, 3 Jun): **49.3** in May (from **52.7** in Apr) — first contraction since Apr 2025; new orders down three months running.
- **Retail sales, Great Britain: April 2026** (ONS, 22 May): volumes **−1.3% m/m** (consensus **−0.6%**); ex-fuel **−0.4%**.

Relative to this softness, the hiking cycle is too front-loaded. Hikes should migrate from 2026 to 2027.

## (2) BOE communication at April MPC

At the April MPC, the BOE laid out three scenarios. In Scenario A, energy prices follow the futures curve, inflation peaks in Q4 2026 at 3.6%, and there are no second-round effects (in regard to inflationary pressures). In Scenario B, energy prices follow the futures curve, headline inflation peaks at 3.7% in Q4 2026, and there are moderate second-round effects. Finally, in Scenario C, energy prices prove more persistent, headline inflation peaks at 6.2% in Q4 2026, and there are much greater second-round effects.

Importantly: in the press conference, Bailey referenced pre-March pricing (~40bps of cuts over the course of 2026), and stated that, given such pricing, most of the changes in the Bank Rate required under Scenarios A and B could "largely be accomodated by reversing out these assumed cuts without further increasing rates". 

In light of this guidance, we deem there to be asymmetry towards steepening of the Dec 27 - Dec 26 curve. That is: Dec 26 has both more room to rally, and less room to sell-off, than does Dec 27. Thus steepening should occur in both the world in which the BOE is repriced hawkishly, over the next few months, and that in which it is repriced dovishly. Meanwhile, should the BOE be adjudged 'on-hold', the curve should see hikes migrate out from 2026 to 2027 - that is, steepening, again. 

In reality, i'd like to implement a swaption bull steepening structure, as I believe asymmetry, in particular, to be towards a dovish repricing of the Bank Rate overall. Yet this shall do for now.
