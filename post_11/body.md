# Optimal expressions of EURHUF downside
 
EURHUF downside has naturally become strongly bid. Expectedly, most of the appetite for EURHUF downside has come in vol-buying expressions, e.g., 6m to 1y tenor digital puts. This has led EURHUF vols to diverge significantly from its FX peers. This begs the question: are there expressions of EURHUF downside that do not entail buying already-rich base vols? Indeed, one might consider an EKO put. 

Consider for instance a 1y 350 put with 330 EKO. Below 350, the structure begins to make money. However, below 330, the entire structure 'knocks-out' - and the payoff is zero. Hence the view being expressed is that HUF will strengthen in a gradual, grinding fashion. Importantly, this expression is not long vol, as overly sharp strengthening of the HUF would lead to EKO. It follows that this structure should enjoy a better carry profile than, say, a 1y 350 digital put.

A 1y 350 digital put _is_ unequivocally long volatility. One wants EURHUF to explode violently to the downside, such that the structure becomes in the money (at which point a certain fixed payout will be realised). The value of the 'long-vol-ness' of a long-volatility structure decays over time, as the time remaining for sharp moves to occur lessens. Hence a 1y 350 digital put will bleed PnL simply in virtue of the passage of time. 

For a 1y 350 digital put to retain its value over time, spot must fall towards the structure's strike. This way, the structure's delta PnL offsets its vega PnL. For the EKO put, however, there is no vol bleed to offset - and yet it is also short delta. Therefore: realisation of the spot path required for the digital put to _maintain_ value, leads the EKO put to in fact _gain_ value.
